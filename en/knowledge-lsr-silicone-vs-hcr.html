1. Overview: Choosing Between LSR and HCR Silicone

When developing precision elastomeric components, design engineers frequently evaluate the choice between LSR (Liquid Silicone Rubber) and HCR (High Consistency Rubber, also known as solid silicone gum). While both materials feature the same durable polysiloxane molecular backbone, their molecular weight, physical viscosity, curing mechanism, and tooling architectures differ substantially.

Selecting the optimal material and process directly impacts tooling budget (NRE), unit production cost, dimensional repeatability, and regulatory compliance. Jun-Hsiang Enterprise operates both precision LIM liquid injection presses and vacuum compression molding machinery in Taiwan, providing transparent, non-biased engineering evaluations for global OEM buyers.

2. Raw Material Rheology & Viscosity Characteristics

The primary physical distinction between LSR and HCR lies in their raw material state and viscosity:

  • Liquid Silicone Rubber (LSR): Supplied as a two-component pumpable paste (Part A and Part B) with a paste-like viscosity typically between 100,000 and 1,000,000 mPa·s. It is mixed dynamically at a 1:1 ratio at ambient temperature and pumped directly into a chilled runner system before entering heated cavities. Its low viscosity allows effortless filling of micro-features, thin walls, and complex core geometries.
  • High Consistency Rubber (HCR): Supplied as high-molecular-weight solid gum stock. It must be plasticized, compounded, and milled on open two-roll mills with curing catalysts, pigments, and functional fillers. The uncured compound is rolled or pre-formed into specific pre-weighed slugs or strips prior to mold placement.

3. Cross-Linking Chemistry: Platinum Addition vs. Peroxide Radical Cure

Curing kinetics fundamentally dictate part cleanliness, biocompatibility, and volatile byproducts:

  • Platinum Addition Cure (Standard in LSR, Optional in HCR): Operates via hydrosilylation between vinyl groups and silicon-hydride cross-linkers catalyzed by platinum complexes. This reaction produces zero volatile organic byproducts, exhibits minimal shrinkage during cure, and offers superior optical clarity and biocompatibility. It complies seamlessly with medical (USP Class VI) and food-contact (FDA 21 CFR 177.2600, LFGB) regulations.
  • Organic Peroxide Cure (Common in HCR): Utilizes organic peroxides (e.g., DCBP, DBPH) to initiate free-radical crosslinking under heat and pressure. The reaction releases acidic decomposition byproducts that typically require a secondary post-cure baking (e.g., 200°C for 2–4 hours) to eliminate volatile organic residues and prevent yellowing or blooming.

4. Tooling Architecture & Molding Process: LIM Injection vs. Compression

The tooling and equipment philosophies between the two processes represent distinct manufacturing disciplines:

Engineering Parameter LSR (Liquid Injection Molding) HCR (Compression / Transfer Molding)
Tooling Architecture Cold runner system with electric heated cavity; precision vacuum venting channels. Heated platen molds with overflow grooves and tear-trim flash lands.
Flash & Parting Line Control Flashless or minimal flash capability due to precision mold shut-offs; reduced post-processing. Requires manual or cryogenic deburring to remove parting line overflow flash.
Cycle Time & Automation Fast vulcanization cycle; automated demolding via robotic arm or air blow. Longer vulcanization cycle; requires manual loading of preforms and manual demolding.
Tooling Investment (NRE) Higher initial investment due to cold runner complexity, vacuum sealing, and high-grade tool steel. Lower initial investment; economical for prototyping, pilot runs, and medium batches.
Overmolding Suitability Excellent for 2-shot or insert overmolding on engineering thermoplastics (PC, PA, PBT) and metals. Best suited for metal inserts capable of withstanding prolonged compression heat and clamp pressure.
Hardness Range Available Shore A 5° to 80° (optimal between 20° and 70°). Shore A 20° to 90° (can reach higher durometer levels).

5. Procurement Decision Framework: When to Choose LSR vs. HCR

To maximize cost-efficiency and part performance, global buyers should balance production volume against precision requirements:

  • Choose LSR Liquid Silicone Injection if:
    • Your project demands high annual volume where automated rapid cycling offsets tooling capital costs.
    • The component features micro-fluidic channels, thin cross-sections, or complex 3D undercut features.
    • You require primerless self-bonding onto delicate thermoplastics without thermal distortion.
    • Strict medical compliance (USP Class VI) or high-transparency optical clarity is required.
  • Choose HCR Solid Compression Molding if:
    • The project is in early-stage development, prototyping, or annual volumes remain modest (< 10,000 pcs).
    • Initial tooling budget (NRE) is tightly constrained.
    • The part is large, heavy, or has a thick wall cross-section (e.g., industrial vibration dampeners, large gaskets).
    • Very high durometer silicone (Shore A 75°–90°) or heavy specialty fillers (conductive carbon, magnetic particles) are necessary.
